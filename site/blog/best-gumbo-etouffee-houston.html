Best Gumbo & Étouffée in Houston

  • Home
  • Blog
  • Best Gumbo & Étouffée in Houston
By Penny September 18, 2026

Gumbo and crawfish étouffée are the two dishes people ask us about most, and for good reason: they are the clearest examples of what Cajun and Creole cooking actually is. Both start the same way at Bayou Fork Kitchen — a roux, cooked low and slow in a heavy pot until it darkens, no shortcuts.

How we make our Gumbo

Our Gumbo starts with flour and fat cooked down to a deep, dark roux, the base that gives gumbo its color and its backbone. From there it is andouille sausage, chicken, shrimp, the holy trinity of onion, celery and bell pepper, and a long simmer before it goes over a scoop of rice. It is the dish most people mean when they picture Louisiana cooking, and it is the one we would tell a first-time customer to start with.

How we make our Crawfish Étouffée

Étouffée means “smothered,” and that is exactly the technique: crawfish tails smothered in a butter-and-flour roux with the trinity, built lighter and richer than gumbo, served over rice with toasted bread on the side. Our Crawfish Étouffée leans Creole — it is the plate we point to when someone asks what “Creole” actually tastes like next to Cajun.

Why you will not find these at a Viet-Cajun boil spot

Houston's Viet-Cajun seafood boil restaurants are a different tradition entirely: shrimp, crawfish or crab boiled and then tossed in a bag with garlic butter and Cajun seasoning, sold by the pound. It is delicious, but it is not built on a roux, and you will not find gumbo or étouffée on a boil menu — those are dishes that take hours of stovetop work, not a quick toss in a bag. If you are searching for the best gumbo or étouffée in Houston specifically, you are looking for a kitchen that cooks a roux, not a boil shop.

How to order

Both dishes are available every day, lunch or dinner portion, for drive-thru, pickup or delivery to Houston, Manvel, Pearland, Missouri City and Sugar Land. Order online, or call the kitchen at 10600 Fondren Road. Same-day orders need to be in by 11.00am.

FAQ: Gumbo & Étouffée in Houston

What is the difference between gumbo and étouffée?

Gumbo is a soup built on a dark roux with sausage, chicken and shrimp, served over rice. Étouffée is thicker and smothered, usually built around crawfish tails in a lighter roux, also served over rice. Gumbo leans Cajun, étouffée leans Creole.

Do you make your roux from scratch?

Yes. Every gumbo and étouffée starts with flour and fat cooked down in a pot at Bayou Fork Kitchen. Nothing is pre-made or shipped in.

Is your gumbo spicy?

It has a real Cajun kick, layered through the roux and seasoning rather than added on top. If you want it milder, ask when you order.

Can I get gumbo or étouffée delivered?

Yes, we deliver to Houston, Manvel, Pearland, Missouri City and Sugar Land. Order online for pickup or delivery.

Is gumbo the same thing as a seafood boil?

No. Gumbo is a roux-based soup cooked for hours. A seafood boil, like Houston's Viet-Cajun style, is shellfish boiled and tossed in seasoned butter, sold by the pound. They are both Louisiana or Gulf Coast inspired, but they are different dishes entirely.
