Houston Food Bloggers & Media: Visit Bayou Fork Kitchen

  • Home
  • Blog
  • Food Bloggers & Media
By Penny October 22, 2026

Houston food writers, restaurant reviewers, bloggers, creators and local media are invited to get to know Bayou Fork Kitchen. We are a Black-owned, family-run Cajun, Creole and Southern comfort food restaurant at 10600 Fondren Road in Southwest Houston. Whether your audience follows new restaurants, neighborhood finds, breakfast, family businesses or Louisiana cooking, we would be glad to help you plan a visit.

Start with our first-timer’s guide for the full restaurant overview, or read on for story ideas, dishes to consider and the best way to arrange an interview or tasting.

Why Bayou Fork may fit your audience

  • A family restaurant story: owner and operator Penny runs the business with her son, executive chef Blake. Their restaurant experience began with Mardi Gras Memphis in 2015 before the family brought its cooking to Houston.
  • A new Southwest Houston kitchen: Bayou Fork opened on Fondren Road in September 2026, serving the Brays Oaks area and nearby Westbury, Meyerland and Sharpstown.
  • More than one service occasion: breakfast begins at 6:30am, the drive-thru is open throughout service, and the same kitchen serves lunch, dinner, pickup, delivery and catering.
  • A distinct point of view: the menu centers plated Cajun and Creole dishes, scratch-made roux, fried seafood, pasta and Southern sides rather than Houston’s familiar Viet-Cajun seafood-boil format. Our guide explains the difference between the two styles.

What to taste and photograph

A review can be built around one dish or a broader table. These menu items show the range of the kitchen:

  • Gumbo: a dark-roux bowl with seafood, sausage and rice.
  • Crawfish Étouffée: a Louisiana classic that shows the kitchen’s approach to roux and layered seasoning.
  • Shrimp & Grits: available from breakfast onward and one of the clearest links between the breakfast and Creole menus.
  • Mardi Gras Pasta: a colorful, creamy Cajun pasta and one of the menu’s most camera-ready plates.
  • Fried Shrimp Po-Boy: fried shrimp, toasted French bread, lettuce, tomato and comeback sauce.
  • The Big Daddy: the signature platter for creators looking to show several of the kitchen’s flavors in one presentation.

See the full menu before your visit. Availability and prices can change, so please confirm any time-sensitive details with the restaurant before publishing.

Story and interview possibilities

Penny can speak about building a family business, moving the restaurant story from Memphis to Houston and serving a new neighborhood. Chef Blake can speak about his professional kitchen background, training at Emeril’s, recipe development and the way Cajun and Creole seasoning is built throughout a dish. Read more about them in our family restaurant story.

Other useful angles include an early drive-thru breakfast in Southwest Houston, the difference between Cajun and Creole cooking, signature gumbo and étouffée, and practical Cajun catering for Houston events.

How to request a visit, tasting or interview

Email bayoufork@gmail.com or use our contact form. To help us respond, include:

  • Your name and publication, channel or social account.
  • Links to recent restaurant or food coverage.
  • Your audience and the story or format you have in mind.
  • Preferred dates and times, the number of people attending and any dietary needs.
  • Whether you would like an interview with Penny or Chef Blake.

Walk-in reviewers are always welcome during regular service. Advance notice is helpful when you need an interview, a larger table, several dishes timed for photography or access beyond the normal guest experience.

Independent and transparent coverage

We welcome honest opinions. A visit, invitation or hosted item is never conditioned on favorable coverage. If Bayou Fork provides anything complimentary, please disclose it to your audience in the way required by your publication or platform. Please also identify yourself before photographing staff members, other guests or non-public areas of the restaurant.
