Cajun Catering Menu Ideas for 10, 25 or 50 Guests

  • Home
  • Blog
  • Cajun Catering Menu Ideas
By Penny October 20, 2026

Short answer: A good Cajun catering menu gives guests a clear main dish, two or more sides, and enough variety for different appetites. Bayou Fork Kitchen builds each Houston catering quote around the actual guest count, venue and service style rather than forcing every event into one package.

The menus below are planning ideas, not fixed packages or guaranteed portions. Use them to decide what sounds right, then send us your date, headcount, location and service needs so we can recommend quantities and prepare a quote.

Cajun catering ideas for about 10 guests

For a family gathering, small office lunch or game-day table, keep the menu focused. Choose one centerpiece such as Sweet Heat Wings, Mardi Gras Pasta, fried seafood or crawfish étouffée. Add two sides such as dirty rice, baked macaroni and cheese, greens, candied yams or green beans.

If the group wants individual meals rather than a shared tray, ask about family-style plates or a mix of menu items. Tell us whether the food is the entire meal or one part of a larger spread because that changes the amount each guest will need.

Cajun catering ideas for about 25 guests

For a birthday, team lunch, shower or graduation gathering, two main choices give guests useful variety. Pair gumbo or étouffée with wings, pasta, fried catfish or shrimp. Dirty rice works naturally with Louisiana dishes, while macaroni and cheese, greens and candied yams round out a Southern comfort menu.

Decide whether guests will serve themselves from a buffet, receive boxed or plated meals, or gather around family-style trays. Buffet service benefits from dishes that hold and portion well. Individual meals can make a short lunch window or workplace delivery easier.

Cajun catering ideas for about 50 guests

For a wedding event, corporate appreciation meal, church gathering or large private party, start with the flow of the room. A buffet may combine gumbo or étouffée, a second main such as chicken, seafood or pasta, several sides and bread. A plated meal needs a more structured selection and more service planning.

At this size, venue access, serving equipment, setup time and dietary needs matter as much as the menu. Tell us when guests will eat, whether the venue has a kitchen or staging area, and whether you need drop-off, buffet setup, family-style service or a full plated meal.

Choose dishes that travel well

  • Louisiana classics: gumbo, crawfish étouffée and dirty rice.
  • Crowd-friendly mains: Sweet Heat Wings, Mardi Gras Pasta, fried seafood and family meal platters.
  • Southern sides: macaroni and cheese, greens, candied yams, green beans, mashed potatoes and baked beans.
  • Service options: drop-off, buffet with setup, family style or plated service, depending on the event.

Brunch and game-day catering

Morning events work too. For a recent Texas Southern vs. Southern University game-day brunch, we served Creole shrimp and grits, waffles with fresh berries, scrambled eggs and bacon from chafing dishes. See the full brunch spread for photos and planning tips.

What we need for an accurate catering quote

Send the date, guest count, venue address, event type, preferred dishes and service style. Include dietary restrictions and food allergies at the start of the conversation. Pork is used in our kitchen, so guests with restrictions should discuss their needs with the catering team before finalizing the menu.
