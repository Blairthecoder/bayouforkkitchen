Houston Office Lunch Catering Guide

  • Home
  • Blog
  • Office Lunch Catering
By Penny September 20, 2026

Planning an office lunch sounds simple until you are balancing headcount, meeting time, dietary needs, serving space and a hungry team. The easiest way to keep it under control is to make five decisions before asking for a quote. This guide will help you plan a Houston office lunch that arrives with the right amount of food and a menu people are excited to eat.

1. Confirm the real headcount

Start with the number of people who will actually eat, not the total number invited. Add anyone who needs an individual meal, then note late arrivals, clients and on-site vendors. Tell the caterer whether the group includes children or especially light appetites. A clear headcount makes portion planning more accurate and keeps you from guessing tray sizes.

2. Match the service style to the meeting

A working lunch usually needs food that can be served quickly and eaten without interrupting the agenda. A buffet is a strong choice for team lunches, trainings and employee appreciation meals because guests can choose their own portions. Individually packaged meals may make more sense when people will eat at different times. For a celebration or client event, setup and presentation may matter as much as speed. Share your schedule and serving space when you request a Houston catering quote.

3. Build a Cajun menu with variety

For a group, choose a familiar main dish, one signature Louisiana dish and sides that balance the meal. Sweet Heat Wings are easy to share. scratch-made Gumbo gives the table a classic Cajun option, while Crawfish Étouffée or Crawfish Fettuccine adds something richer. Dirty rice and mac and cheese help round out a buffet. Browse the full Cajun and Creole menu before choosing your favorites.

4. Ask about allergies before the order is final

Send one message to the team asking about allergies and dietary restrictions, then give the caterer the details in writing. Be specific about shellfish, fish, dairy and pork. Bayou Fork Kitchen uses pork in the kitchen, and many Cajun dishes include seafood, dairy or sausage, so advance notice helps the kitchen explain which choices may work for your group. Guests with serious allergies should always speak directly with the restaurant before ordering.

5. Plan the handoff

Provide the full address, suite or floor, a contact phone number and the time food should be ready to serve. Mention parking, loading instructions, security desks and elevators. If you are picking up, leave enough time to return, set out the food and let everyone get settled before the meeting begins. Your office catering request checklist

  • Event date and serving time
  • Confirmed guest count
  • Office address and delivery instructions
  • Pickup, drop-off, buffet setup or full service
  • Budget and menu preferences
  • Allergies and dietary restrictions
  • Plates, utensils, serving tools and beverages needed
