What is shrimp and grits?

Shrimp and grits is a Southern dish of shrimp served over grits, which are coarsely ground corn cooked slowly until soft and creamy. It traces back to the Lowcountry of coastal South Carolina and Georgia, where it started as a simple breakfast in shrimping communities: fresh shrimp cooked in butter or bacon fat and served over a bowl of grits.

Over the last few decades it moved from home kitchens onto restaurant menus across the South. Every region now has its own take, and you will see versions with cheese grits, tomato gravy, bacon or sausage.

Lowcountry vs. Creole shrimp and grits

The easiest way to tell the two styles apart is the sauce.

  • Lowcountry style: Usually simpler. Shrimp cooked in butter or bacon drippings, sometimes with a light pan gravy, lemon or green onion. The shrimp and the grits do most of the talking.
  • Creole style: Richer and more seasoned. Louisiana kitchens tend to build a cream or butter sauce with Creole seasoning, and often the onion, celery and bell pepper base Louisiana cooks call the “holy trinity.” The sauce soaks into the grits.

Ours is the Creole kind. The same cream-sauce tradition runs through a lot of our menu, so if shrimp and grits is your thing, you are in the right kitchen. If you want the bigger picture on the two Louisiana traditions, read Cajun or Creole? What’s actually on your plate.
