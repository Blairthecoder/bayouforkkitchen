Étouffée, Gumbo & Jambalaya

  • Home
  • Blog
  • Étouffée vs. Gumbo vs. Jambalaya
By Penny August 19, 2026

Three Louisiana classics get mixed up constantly, and honestly, it is fair — they all start with a roux and end up over rice. But each one is built differently, and once you know what to look for, you will taste the difference immediately.

Étouffée: smothered, not soupy

Étouffée means “smothered.” It is a thick, roux-based sauce built around one protein — crawfish, in our case — and ladled over rice. Less liquid than gumbo, more like a rich stew than a soup. Our Crawfish Étouffée is the clearest example on our menu.

Gumbo: darker roux, more in the pot

Gumbo starts with a darker, longer-cooked roux and turns into something closer to a hearty soup or stew, usually loaded with more than one protein — seafood and sausage together is classic. It is Cajun at its root, built for feeding a crowd from one pot. Try our Gumbo to taste the difference in the roux.

Jambalaya: the rice cooks in the pot

Jambalaya is the odd one out: the rice is not served under the dish, it is cooked directly in the pot with the meat, seafood and seasoning, so it soaks up all that flavor as it cooks. We do not currently carry jambalaya as a standalone dish, but you will taste the same building blocks — roux, sausage, seafood, layered spice — across our Gumbo and Crawfish Étouffée.

Which one to order first

New to all three? Start with the étouffée if you want something rich and direct, or the gumbo if you want a deeper, more complex pot with more going on. Either way, get the dirty rice on the side and add a hit of our house hot sauce if you like it to keep working on you through the whole plate.
